Blackout Protocol

A small top-down strategy game about a single outpost and never enough power. Keep the generator alive through three attack waves by deciding, second by second, which lights, turret and repair drone get to run.

01

Design

Tower defense games usually ask where to build. Blackout Protocol fixes every building in place and asks a different question: what can you afford to keep running right now? Everything draws from the same small power budget, and the map is pitch black wherever a lamp is off.

Design pillars

P1

Power is the currency

The generator delivers 10 units. Four lamps (1 each), the turret (4) and the repair drone (3) add up to 11 – something always stays off.

P2

Light defines the battlefield

The turret only targets enemies standing in light. Every lamp switched off to feed the drone opens a blind spot.

P3

Recover, don’t restart

Destroyed structures leave ruins that can be rebuilt between waves with scrap dropped by enemies.

Core loop

  1. Preparation: rebuild, plan power→
  2. Wave: reroute power mid-attack→
  3. Turret clears lit enemies, drone repairs→
  4. Wave cleared, scrap collected↺ repeat
  1. Enemy reaches a dark spot→
  2. Lamps and turret fall→
  3. Generator destroyed = blackout

Key decisions

Light as a rule, not just a look

Each lamp’s light radius doubles as a gameplay query. Before targeting, the turret asks whether a position is lit, so what players see and what the rules do can’t drift apart.

A* on an occupancy grid

Eight directions, no corner cutting, octile heuristic. Structures occupy their cells, enemies path to the free cell next to their target, and a mover re-plans the moment its next cell gets blocked.

Load shedding

When capacity drops, the PowerManager switches off the most recently powered consumers first until usage fits the budget again – predictable for players, simple to reason about.

Behaviour as data

Crawler and Breaker share one EnemyController state machine. They differ only in their data assets: one targets the nearest structure, the other goes straight for the generator, with a fallback strategy if it’s gone.

Ruins by deactivation

A destroyed structure releases its grid cell and power, then is disabled instead of deleted. Rebuilding reactivates the same object at full health – no prefab references, no lost setup.

One owner for the game flow

The GameManager owns the phases Preparation, Wave, Won and Lost. HUD, music and results screen only listen to its events; pause runs on timeScale while UI and music fades use unscaled time.

03

Iteration

What playtesting showed – and what changed because of it.

  1. Iteration 01 · A darkness that wasn’t dark

    Observed
    Even with a very dim ambient light, enemies stayed readable outside the lamps. Switching a lamp off barely felt like a cost.
    Changed
    Removed ambient light entirely. Only powered lamps and the generator light the map.
    Result
    Every lamp you turn off now creates a blind spot you can see – the core decision gained real weight.

  2. Iteration 02 · An undefendable generator

    Observed
    The generator sat in darkness. Since the turret only hits lit targets, a single Breaker could walk up and destroy it unopposed.
    Changed
    The generator now emits a faint light of its own. Light sources without a power cost count as always on.
    Result
    The last line of defence can be held, while the lamps still decide how early enemies get intercepted.

  3. Iteration 03 · Lost long before game over

    Observed
    Once a lamp was destroyed, its area stayed dark for the rest of the mission. Runs were effectively lost minutes before the generator fell.
    Changed
    Destroyed structures leave ruins. During the preparation phase they can be rebuilt with scrap that enemies drop.
    Result
    Mistakes are recoverable, and scrap adds a second decision between waves: what gets rebuilt first.
